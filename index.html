About Me

I earned my PhD in Astronomy from New Mexico State University. My research focuses on galaxy evolution, with special interests in the timescales over which Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) evolve and impart feedback on their host galaxies and environment, an area known as AGN Archaeology, as well as the cosmic co-evolution of AGN and their host galaxies. I use radio, infrared, and optical observations to identify AGN, constrain the timescales associated with their different feedback (accretion) modes, and study the properties of their host galaxies.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Researcher in Extragalactic Astrophysics at Haverford College. In this role, I am assisting both the Roman Science Collaboration and the Galaxy Zoo collaborations to setting up the first Roman Galaxy Zoo project!

Research

My research investigates how supermassive black holes evolve and influence their host galaxies. By combining multiwavelength observations, I study the signatures of past and ongoing black hole activity.

  • AGN & Black Hole Evolution

    I use extended emission-line nebulae and other tracers of ionized gas to investigate how active galactic nuclei (AGN) change over time, including the fading signatures of past quasar activity.

  • Radio Galaxies & Jets

    I study radio emission from jets and lobes to understand the history of black hole activity. Radio interferometry helps reveal the structure, evolution, and timescales of jet-driven emission.

  • Galaxy Evolution

    I combine optical, infrared, and radio observations to explore the relationship between black holes, their host galaxies, and the surrounding gas. My work focuses on galaxies hosting extended emission-line nebulae and radio jets.
